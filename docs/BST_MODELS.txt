Five BST models: definitions, comparisons, and online scope

The entry point is DynamicOptimality/BSTModels.lean. Unless qualified
otherwise, theorem names below belong to namespace DynamicOptimality.
Let n be the number of keys, X a finite sequence of successful requests
in {0,...,n-1}, and m its length. All actual trees use precisely these
keys. Computation and mathematical bookkeeping are free. A rotation
means one ordinary left or right BST rotation. Depth counts edges from
the root, so the root has depth zero.

The shared OPT X is the attained minimum of m plus the rotation count
among root-serving executions, with a freely chosen initial tree.
The new comparisons reuse this OPT; they introduce no replacement optimum.
See DynamicOptimality/Cost.lean and ModelCostEquivalence.lean.

The five models

1. Root service. Before each request, perform ordinary rotations until
   its key is at the root. Charge one per rotation and one for service.
   Rotations may occur at any subtree. The execution interface ends at
   the last service; trailing rotations are unnecessary for its cost
   comparison. Definition: ModelCosts.root in ModelCostEquivalence.lean.

2. Search followed by rotations. Search the tree as it stands before
   this access, paying depth + 1. Then perform any finite rotation
   sequence, paying its actual length. That final tree is the next
   access's initial tree. Definition: SearchRotation.Access in
   DynamicOptimality/SearchRotationModel.lean; ModelCosts.search.

3. Adjacent pointer. Start each access with a pointer at the current
   root. Each move to a parent or child, each rotation at the pointer,
   and serving the requested key costs one. The pointer must be at the
   requested key when serving it. Its final position is unrestricted;
   the next access starts at the resulting tree's root. This counts
   actual operations, including repeated movements. Definition:
   ChaCha.MTR.Pointer.Access in
   chacha/lean/ChaCha/PointerModel.lean; ModelCosts.pointer.

4. Rooted hull. Select a connected collection of nodes containing the
   root and the requested key. Replace their tree by any BST on those
   same keys, preserving every untouched hanging subtree in its inorder
   gap. Here a gap is a position between consecutive selected keys, or
   before the first or after the last key. Charge the number of selected
   nodes. The final root is unrestricted. Definition:
   ChaCha.MTR.HullAccess in
   chacha/lean/ChaCha/BSTExecution.lean; ModelCosts.hull.

5. Geometry. Positive-time row t contains the t-th request and any other
   touched keys.
   A touch is the point (key,t), and the cost is the number of points
   in positive-time rows. The point set is arborally satisfied: every
   closed axis-aligned rectangle whose opposite corners are two points
   in different rows and columns contains a third point, possibly on
   its boundary. The definition contains no assumed BST realization.
   Definitions and the exact finite-point characterization are in
   DynamicOptimality/GeometricModel.lean: Geometry.ArborallySatisfied,
   Geometry.Execution, and Geometry.execution_iff_points.

The geometric proofs also use a last-touch surface: height(key) is the
most recent row containing that key. An uncharged horizontal row at
time zero supplies a surface for previously untouched keys. The theorem
Geometry.baseline_union_iff proves that adding this row imposes no extra
rectangle constraint on positive-time points. It does not prescribe a
physical initial BST. Geometry.execution_iff_surface connects the local
surface condition to the ordinary point-set definition.

Finite cost equivalence

For a model M, let a and b mean the following two statements:
every M-execution of cost c satisfies OPT X <= a*c; and some M-execution
of X has cost at most b*OPT X. Constants are uniform in n and X.

    Model          a     b
    Root           1     1
    Search         2     2
    Pointer       13    37
    Hull          13     1
    Geometry      13     1

ModelCosts.{root,search,pointer,hull}Comparison are in
DynamicOptimality/ModelCostEquivalence.lean; geometryComparison is in
DynamicOptimality/GeometricCost.lean. BSTModel.transfer gives factor
b(target)*a(source) for each ordered pair. BSTModel.all_five_equivalent
gives the convenient common factor 481 for all 25 pairs. These finite
existence statements permit freely chosen initial trees and dependence
on the complete request sequence; they do not preserve final trees.
They include the empty universe and empty request sequence.

Hull and geometry have exactly the same attainable costs, not merely
comparable minima: ModelCosts.geometry_iff_hull in GeometricCost.lean.
The forward proof records the selected hull keys in each row. The reverse
proof reconstructs trees backwards, repairing the selected part of a
max-heap: a tree whose parent's last-touch time is at least every
descendant's. These choices may depend on later rows. See
Geometry.of_hullExecution in GeometricFromBST.lean and
Geometry.to_hullExecution in GeometricToBST.lean.

BSTModel.competitive_iff replaces the competitor's model in any shared
Competitive statement. It preserves the algorithm's stated cost and
competitive factor, changing only the universal constant.

Prescribed initial tree

DynamicOptimality/ModelInitialization.lean proves actual initialization
using at most 6*n rotations and gives these witnesses from any supplied
BST, with a single startup allowance:

    root_from_initial       OPT X + 6*n
    search_from_initial   2*OPT X + 12*n
    hull_from_initial       OPT X + 6*n
    pointer_from_initial 37*OPT X + 222*n

The first search in the search model is charged in the supplied tree,
before initialization rotations. The empty word performs no setup.

Online conversions

An online strategy fixes its successor state and operations from the
current state and request, before future requests are supplied.
DynamicOptimality/ModelOnline.lean packages the three additional tree
strategies and proves prefix consistency with OnlineModel.run_append.
Its local conversions preserve both boundary trees: root-to-search has
factor 2, hull-to-pointer 37, pointer-to-hull 1, and hull/pointer-to-search
25. Their theorem names are root_to_search, hull_to_pointer,
pointer_to_hull, hull_to_search, and pointer_to_search in OnlineModel.

ModelOnlineNormalization.lean converts any OnlineBST to root, hull, and
pointer strategies with factors 2, 2, and 74 on every finite prefix.
The source's initial physical tree is preserved, with no startup charge.
Later physical trees may differ: a stored sequence of pending rotations
relates the simulator's tree to the source's virtual tree. Its length is
a nonnegative potential, discharged by root_run_potential. Public bounds
are OnlineModel.Normalization.{root,hull,pointer}_cost_le_from_state.
All choices depend only on the current state and request.

DynamicOptimality/GeometricOnline.lean defines the pure geometric
OnlineStrategy. Geometry.OnlineHull.run_cost preserves hull cost exactly;
Geometry.OnlineSearch.run_cost_le gives factor 2 from any OnlineBST.
The actual Greedy algorithm is Geometry.OnlineGreedy.strategy in
GeometricOnlineGreedy.lean. Its run_rows and initialized_cost theorems
retain the existing Greedy rows and cost. Clamping negative initial
heights to zero proves feasibility without changing those chosen rows.

Deferred: an arbitrary online geometric strategy has not yet been
converted to an online BST at constant overhead. The backwards offline
reconstruction cannot supply that stronger quantifier order. An online
proof must select each physical transition before later request rows are
known. The current results therefore do not export a physical online
Greedy BST.
